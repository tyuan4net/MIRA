# MIRA Vision

Date: 2026-06-16

## What MIRA Is Reaching Toward

MIRA is a human atlas.

It is not meant to be introduced first as an astrology tool, a Human Design
tool, a Bazi tool, a Ziwei tool, or any fixed divination/report system. Those
can become layers later. The deeper shape of MIRA is an open query platform
for observing human life patterns across time, place, symbolic systems, life
events, tags, notes, relationships, communities, and history.

One end connects to the user's observation point: the question someone wants
to ask, the pattern they suspect, the life change they want to compare, the
birth time they want to narrow, or the record they want to preserve.

One end connects to scattered systems of calculation and meaning. At first,
that may include astrology and Human Design. Later, it may include Bazi,
Ziwei, or other systems. MIRA should treat them as queryable layers, not as
the whole identity of the project.

One end connects to human life in the world: early death, childhood illness,
turbulent lives, migration, poverty and wealth, marriage and children, work,
vocation, historical disruption, large-scale period features, geography,
settlement, community, affinity, and encounter.

The wish is to build software where these ends can meet without being locked
inside one school, one commercial product, or one fixed interpretation.

## Open Foundations

MIRA's foundation should remain explicit and open.

Time and place are not just input fields. They are research material.

MIRA should allow civil time zones, administrative daylight saving rules,
geographical time, geographical time plus equation-of-time style correction,
precise GPS location, open true/mean variants, custom celestial bodies,
custom virtual points, custom symbolic relations, and user-defined names for
patterns, aspects, points, or entities.

The user should be able to ask what changes when a convention changes.

This matters because a life pattern may depend on a minute of birth time, a
location convention, a true/mean choice, a house calculation, or a user's own
definition table. MIRA should preserve those choices rather than hiding them.

## Query As The Center

MIRA should be query-first.

It should not assume that the final output is a built-in report. A user may
want to search, compare, annotate, display, export, or define an entirely new
view.

Users should eventually be able to define:

- their own tags and tag trees
- their own symbolic tables
- their own relationship rules
- their own names for configurations
- their own output formats and reports
- their own displayers or presentation surfaces
- their own research notes and search paths

This is why MIRA should feel less like a closed application and more like an
open human-pattern studio.

## Human Records

MIRA can also become a place to preserve human records.

People may store birth information, share it with others, annotate life
events, keep research notes, compare different periods, and search through
hard-won observations.

This gives MIRA a second shape:

> It is not only a calculator. It can become an interactive research notebook.

And perhaps:

> MIRA can become an interactive electronic book of human lives.

## Open Inquiry

MIRA should leave room for disagreement.

Some people may treat symbolic systems as calculation tools. Some may treat
them as cultural artifacts. Some may use MIRA only as a historical,
biographical, or data-organizing platform. Some may stand before the same data
and feel that life has pattern, rhythm, or invitation.

The software should not force an answer.

It should allow careful observation, parameter changes, comparison, doubt,
wonder, and renewed attention.

## Ethical Direction

If MIRA ever helps people sense that life has a pattern, the response should
not be control, fear, or fatalism. The better response is humility and care:

- to protect one's body and mind
- to treat other people gently
- to experience the opportunities and encounters that arrive
- to remain honest about uncertainty
- to avoid using symbolic systems to imprison oneself or others

MIRA should support inquiry without pretending to own life.

## Step By Step

This vision may take years. It may never be fully completed. The project should
move toward it gradually:

1. Keep the small command-line calculators reliable.
2. Keep the astronomical backend interface clean.
3. Keep personal and public human files separate.
4. Keep historical, geographical, symbolic, and biographical data extensible.
5. Keep custom definitions, outputs, reports, and displayers possible.
6. Keep the system open enough that future researchers and users can ask new
   questions that the first author did not imagine.

MIRA is a way to keep looking.
